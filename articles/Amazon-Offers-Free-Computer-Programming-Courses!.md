Today I'd like to share a computer programming course that Amazon recently made available to the public for free: Amazon Future Engineer.

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/uDRkMWLia28hfW1zkDpw70NSgpYjiaS5OBeSHJWlVGQLmZvDIaibJkglTQB6Mcwj5t8CjkO3oXmYLj2uJTiapPKFbA/640)

A few weeks ago, Amazon CEO Jeff Bezos published his annual shareholder letter, in which he discussed some of the measures and efforts Amazon is taking in response to the current pandemic.

In that letter, Bezos also touched on resources that Amazon would open up to the public, and among them was the computer course Xiao G is sharing with you today.

Last year, Amazon officially launched a program called Amazon Future Engineer.

The main goal of this program is to help the next generation of young people acquire the skills they need to thrive in a technology-driven society, and to provide low-income individuals and computer enthusiasts with a more affordable way to learn general computer science skills.

To that end, they also set a small goal: to help hundreds of thousands of people learn computer programming through this program each year.

After the program launched, Amazon provided computer science curriculum sponsorships to more than 2,000 schools across the United States and offered 100 talented students $40,000 university scholarship slots. In addition, these students would also have internship opportunities to learn and work at Amazon.

The website also hosts some free computer learning resources that are open to the public:

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/uDRkMWLia28hfW1zkDpw70NSgpYjiaS5OBhQkezILAZTUSKFGeWGUZH9uPXZ4aUftE8YXGAbJgicbyoibfaibV35NKg/640)

As you can see from the image above, it mainly consists of three major courses:

- Virtual Robotics
- Computer Science Courses
- Celebrity Song Remix

The first course is fairly simple, as it primarily serves students in grades 2-12, so the learning difficulty isn't very high.

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/uDRkMWLia28hfW1zkDpw70NSgpYjiaS5OBHgnSyOLphKIRlBhLEib7TAsoT2ccv0IkPnnLbZJ5EkTm1NJiazxmVDQA/640)

I took a quick look, and it mainly uses a Scratch-like drag-and-drop approach. Students drag and snap program blocks together to make a robot move toward designated targets and complete tasks.

This stage is mainly about helping you learn how to think about the essence of how things operate using a computer science mindset.

The second and third courses are relatively more professional computer programming courses. Course 2 is based on Python and introduces various computer science concepts. Course 3 teaches you how to learn coding through music creation.

Given your current programming skills, the third course should be the most fun and the easiest to learn.

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/uDRkMWLia28j1SPlN5fSL1W0gUvglMuN8BUkatYEic6icSNzq1PuXLoxJYqBd7ZqaLdeCw5V95skBPhlA6AGRo5zw/640)
