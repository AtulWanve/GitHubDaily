Today I'd like to share an interesting computer programming book I came across on Hacker News yesterday: *CS Unplugged*, which literally translates to *Computer Science Without a Computer*.

As you might guess from the title, this book lets you understand fundamental concepts of computer science without needing a computer or writing any code.

How is that possible? Let me explain.

### An Engaging Computer Science Teaching Activity

This book is actually adapted from a public-service educational activity called CS Unplugged, which originated overseas.

The activity has been running for over twenty years. It was initially created by three professors—Tim Bell, Ian Witten, and Mike Fellows—from different universities. Today, it has grown considerably, with support from the University of Canterbury and Google.

The original goal of this initiative was to offer a more accessible and friendly teaching approach, allowing students and professionals from other fields who are interested in computer science to easily appreciate the charm of the discipline.

In everyday teaching sessions, instructors use props like cards, strings, crayons, and puzzles to organize pre-designed educational games.

Through these games, students gradually and naturally pick up foundational computer science knowledge such as binary numbers, algorithms, and data compression.

Below is a 3-minute demo video to give you a feel for what makes this teaching activity unique.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KOYy4kyLEHs&feature=youtu.be

As you can see from the video, the benefit of this approach is that it greatly enhances student engagement, makes specific knowledge points more intuitive and fun, and encourages interaction.

Now, more than twenty years later, CS Unplugged has deeply influenced people of all ages and backgrounds around the world, making an indelible contribution to the promotion of computer science.

If you'd like to learn more about this project, you can visit the following page:

https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-98355-4_29

### Computer Science Without a Computer

As mentioned earlier, this activity has been carried out simultaneously across multiple countries and regions, so the corresponding book is also available in several languages.

In addition to the English edition, the book has been translated into Arabic, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Traditional Chinese, and other languages.

Let's take the Traditional Chinese version as an example and walk through what the book covers.

The authors compiled the content of 21 teaching activities and divided them into four parts. Here's what each part contains:

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_jpg/uDRkMWLia28hR7ISjk5UWwRuibia4qPJxXHu5bQMoibLTx9ThWzLULlsOQ4X6qYODAUbicgCzK7IrmWtQlEEaial54wA/640)

Before each activity, the authors thoughtfully list the lesson summary, curriculum connections, skills learned, recommended age, and required materials, so readers can prepare in advance.

The other items are fairly self-explanatory, so I won't go into detail here. Let's focus on the "Required Materials" section.

As mentioned earlier, many of these activities require props like cards, candles, strings, and puzzles. The "Required Materials" section tells you what to prepare ahead of each lesson.

For example, in the chapter on binary numbers, you need to prepare a set of cards like the ones shown below before starting:

![img](https://mmbiz.qpic.cn/mmbiz_png/uDRkMWLia28hR7ISjk5UWwRuibia4qPJxXHFICcHAWkkqpKyEptQI6YA095u2hqZWLiczTU2gOzSgRmLKBmoQOFKRA/640)

Then, using these cards, you can start learning binary numbers.
